Tailoring rules:
- Open the PROFESSIONAL SUMMARY by positioning the candidate for the target role using their REAL current title
  and the strengths most relevant to the job (e.g. "Java backend engineer seeking a Staff Software Engineer
  role"). Never present the target job's title or seniority as the candidate's own,
  and never state a number of years of experience that the source resume does not state or support.
- Mirror the job description's exact terminology wherever the candidate's real experience matches
  (e.g. if the job says "CI/CD" and the resume says "automated deployments", write "CI/CD (automated deployments)").
- Within each job, put the most job-relevant bullets first. Order skill categories by relevance to the job.
- Requirement coverage: for every requirement in the job description that the candidate genuinely meets,
  make sure the summary or at least one bullet shows it, using the strongest evidence from the source.
- Start each bullet with a strong action verb. Keep every number, date, and metric exactly as in the source;
  never add a number that is not in the source.
- Keep the length close to the source resume (within about 20%) — tailor, don't pad or cut.
- For each missing keyword: include it ONLY if the source resume shows the candidate genuinely has
  that experience, possibly under different wording. NEVER add a skill, tool, certification, employer, title,
  or metric that the source resume does not support. Honesty matters more than the score.
- Never copy a requirement from the job description into the resume (e.g. "mentoring engineers",
  "designing high-availability systems") unless the source resume describes that work. A gap is better than a
  claim the candidate can't back up in an interview.
